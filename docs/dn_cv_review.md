# Dreadnought / Carrier design review (ChatGPT-5.6 Sol, high effort, 2026-09-01)

Reviewed flat-shaded Tinkercad-colour renders of the Yamato-class Dreadnought and
Enterprise-class Carrier 2.1 against the Frigate, Heavy Cruiser and Battleship.

## 1. Silhouette readability

**Yamato dreadnought:** The spinal cannon immediately communicates “capital killer” at normal size. At sprite scale, however, the five nacelles and numerous round fittings merge into a busy engine cluster. Without the cannon, it reads as an upgraded Battleship rather than a separate class. It is distinct from the Heavy Cruiser, but only moderately distinct from the Battleship.

**Enterprise carrier:** The wide outriggers, reduced saucer, and sparse armament give it the more distinctive silhouette. It reads as a carrier at normal size, but the cylindrical flight pods can resemble oversized engines at sprite scale. Flattening those pods would make the role unmistakable.

## 2. Design-language consistency

- Both retain the faction palette and established globe/saucer, nacelle, sensor-dish, turret, and window vocabulary.
- Yamato is almost *too* consistent: it feels like “Battleship plus cannon and more engines.” Its dense collection of domes, rings, and pink endcaps weakens the hierarchy of the main weapon.
- Enterprise introduces a useful new silhouette without leaving the faction. Its main inconsistency is that the silver cylindrical outriggers read more like exposed machinery than blue plate-hull flight decks.
- On both, weapons should use orange more decisively; several small gold/orange details currently compete with the sensor dish.

## 3. Prioritised changes

**Yamato — maximum five**

1. Give the spinal cannon a stronger continuous mounting/spine into the central hull; it currently feels somewhat attached to the command globe.
2. Organize the five nacelles into clearly separated tiers or pairs with stronger negative space, preserving the count without creating a bundle.
3. Lower or armor the globe command pod so the forward silhouette differs from the Battleship.
4. Put the three turrets into an obvious dorsal sequence and the three tubes into a readable symmetrical battery.
5. Remove or enlarge the smallest fittings; anything below turret scale will become visual noise in the sprite.

**Enterprise — maximum five**

1. Flatten the outrigger pods into unmistakable flight-deck slabs, with broad blue upper surfaces.
2. Add large side-facing hangar mouths and short white landing-light banks; these will survive reduction better than fine deck markings.
3. Increase the gap between outriggers and the central hull to create a strong, readable H-shaped silhouette.
4. Broaden and flatten the saucer slightly so it reads as a command/flight deck rather than a squashed globe.
5. Keep the single turret conspicuously orange and isolated; avoid additional weapon-like domes that undermine the carrier’s low-gun identity.

## 4. Flagship choice

**Enterprise better earns the second flagship slot visually.** Its saucer-and-outrigger outline supplies a new role, profile, and focal structure beside the Battleship. Yamato communicates raw power more immediately, but currently reads as the Battleship’s heavily armed derivative rather than an equally iconic counterpart.
