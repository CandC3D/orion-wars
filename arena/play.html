HOW A TURN WORKS

  1. Power. Every ship's power pool refills. A spinal capacitor draws its charge. Your shield reserve is held back for absorption; the rest is for movement and guns.
  2. Impacts. Missiles launched last turn arrive now: point defence may intercept them, small hulls may evade, the rest hit.
  3. Three action rounds. Each round, initiative is rolled and every ship takes one action: move as planned (turn up to its turn rate, then run forward along its facing), or hold and fire every weapon that bears on a target in range. A carrier's strike wave goes in during round 1. Wrecks detonate at the end of each round.
  4. End. The turn closes; missiles fired this turn are in flight until next turn's impacts.

So your plan for a ship is three actions. Move in the rounds you need to reach a firing position; hold in the rounds you want the guns to speak. The fire solution in the vessel panel tells you which weapons will bear on your target after your planned moves.

The scripted opponent lives under exactly the same rules: one action per ship per round.
